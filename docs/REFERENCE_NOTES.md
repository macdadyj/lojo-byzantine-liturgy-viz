# Reference notes: how sael.net is made

Studied on 2026-09-29 from the public pages themselves: the HTML source, the network requests of a
headless Chromium session, and the shipped (minified) JavaScript of Token Town, Inside an AI Data
Center, Model Museum (`/ai-museum`), Set the Mood (`/interior`), and dat.city. Also read: Ryan Sael's
CV (ryansael.com) and his public posts on X. Screenshots were taken for study only and are not
committed. No code, art, or asset from those pages is copied into this repository; the techniques
below are re-implemented from scratch in our own code.

The sael.net home page says the explainers are "becoming an open project" ("Open source, soon"). As of
this date there is no published license, so nothing of his may be reused verbatim.

## The stack

| Piece | What he uses |
| --- | --- |
| Engine | Plain Three.js (r183 on sael.net, r180 on dat.city). No React, no React Three Fiber, no game engine. |
| Build | Each sael.net scene is one self-contained HTML file: an import map points `three` and `three/addons/` at jsDelivr, and all scene code is one inline minified ES module (100 to 230 KB). dat.city is an Astro site with bundled chunks. |
| Assets | None downloaded for the 3D scenes. No glTF/GLB, no Draco, no KTX2, no HDRI, no texture files. Every model is built in code from primitives; every texture (signs, labels, screens, logos) is drawn at runtime on a `<canvas>` (`CanvasTexture`). Only a poster image and a looping preview video per card on the index page. |
| Three.js addons | `EffectComposer`, `GTAOPass`, `UnrealBloomPass`, `OutputPass`, sometimes `SMAAPass`; `RoundedBoxGeometry`, `mergeGeometries`, `ConvexGeometry`, `SVGLoader` (logos); dat.city also uses `BatchedMesh`, `MeshToonMaterial`, AgX tone mapping and the `Sky` shader. All MIT, all part of three.js. |
| UI | Hand-written HTML/CSS over the canvas: Inter from Google Fonts, dark translucent "glass" cards with backdrop blur, rounded pill buttons, a numbered chapter pager ("1 / 14", Previous / Next), small pill labels pinned to objects. dat.city uses Phosphor icons (MIT). |
| Authoring | Per his own posts, the scenes were generated by Claude (Opus 5.5) agents in long single runs, with access to reference libraries and paid APIs (he names SD 2.5 image generation and ElevenLabs audio). We have neither and do not need them: none of the 3D content depends on a generated asset. |

## How he makes people and crowds

This is the part that matters most for us, and it is simpler than it looks.

1. **A person is a handful of primitives merged into one geometry.** In Token Town each figure is about
   14 small boxes: two legs, two feet, a torso, two arms, two hands, a head, a hair cap, a fringe,
   and two dot eyes. In Model Museum the same idea is rounder: a squashed capsule torso, a
   cylinder pelvis, a neck, a sphere head, a half-sphere hair cap, capsule thighs, shins and arms,
   sphere hands and box feet. No skeleton, no skinning, no downloaded model.
2. **Every vertex carries a few numbers**: which body part it belongs to (pants, shirt, skin, hair,
   shoes, eyes), how much it swings when walking (+1 or −1 for the left and right limbs, 0 for the
   torso), and the height of the joint it swings around (hip or shoulder).
3. **The whole crowd is one `InstancedMesh`.** Each instance gets its own shirt, trouser, hair and
   skin colors (small curated palettes) and an animation vector (phase, walking or not) as instanced
   attributes. Heights vary by a per-instance scale of roughly ±8%.
4. **Animation happens in the vertex shader.** A patched `MeshStandardMaterial` (`onBeforeCompile`)
   rotates each limb about its pivot by `sin(time + phase) × swing`, adds a small bob, and picks the
   vertex color from the part id and the instance palette. Moving people just get a new instance
   matrix each frame. Thousands of people cost one draw call.
5. **Soft blob shadows.** One instanced quad per person with a radial gradient texture at about 40%
   opacity, drawn after the crowd, plus the real directional shadow map for the big shapes.
6. **Style, not realism.** Heads are simple spheres or cubes with dot eyes or no face at all. The
   figures read as people through proportion, posture, color and grouping (queues face the door,
   people sit on benches), not detail.

## The look

- **Materials**: almost everything is `MeshStandardMaterial` with a flat color and roughness about
  0.8, cached by color. Gold, glass and metal get `MeshPhysicalMaterial`. Edges are softened with
  `RoundedBoxGeometry` so key light catches a bevel. Static props are merged per material into a few
  big meshes.
- **Custom shader chunk on every material**: an array of about 10 "fake" point lights (the nearest
  practical lamps, re-sorted by distance and strength) is added to the diffuse term in the fragment
  shader, so dozens of lamps, windows and screens light their surroundings without the cost of real
  lights. The same chunk has a per-material saturation and a warm "lift" control.
- **Lights**: one warm directional key with a 2048 PCF shadow map, shadows re-rendered only when
  something changes (`shadowMap.autoUpdate = false`); a hemisphere light for the fill; sometimes a
  cool rim light; a `RoomEnvironment` PMREM at low intensity for reflections.
- **Tone and post**: ACES filmic tone mapping (exposure about 0.95 to 1.12). The composer renders the
  scene into a half-float MSAA target, then: GTAO ambient occlusion at half resolution with the
  Poisson denoiser, `UnrealBloomPass` at half resolution with a high threshold (0.95 to 1.3) so only
  true emitters glow, a custom depth-of-field pass (circle of confusion from the depth buffer, a
  focus distance and an in-focus range, stronger blur in front than behind) that gives the miniature
  "diorama" feel, then `OutputPass`. Exponential fog (`FogExp2`) in a desaturated scene color.
- **Camera**: narrow field of view (30° to 32° in Token Town, the Data Center and Set the Mood; 50° in
  the walkable Museum), a composed three-quarter view from slightly above, damped orbit, and smooth
  eased moves between chapters.
- **Performance**: pixel ratio capped at 1.5 and scaled down so the frame is at most about 2.5 million
  pixels, adaptive quality, instancing everywhere, shadows updated on demand.

## What we take, and what we do not

| Technique | Free? | What we do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Procedural primitive people, one instanced draw, vertex-shader animation | Yes (our own code on three.js, MIT) | Replace the Quaternius GLB cast entirely with our own procedural figures built the same way, including vestments. |
| Per-instance palettes, blob shadows | Yes | Keep and extend (skin, hair, top, bottom, headscarf, vestment colors). |
| Fake practical lights in a material chunk | Yes | Candles, lampadas and chandeliers light people and walls through a shared uniform array. |
| GTAO + bloom + depth of field + ACES | Yes. We use the equivalent MIT effects in `postprocessing` (pmndrs), which we already ship: N8AO, Bloom, DepthOfField. | Tune to his settings: half-res AO, high bloom threshold, focus on the subject of each step. |
| Rounded bevels, flat-color materials | Yes | Rounded pews and furniture; keep icons as the only photographic images, because they are the content. |
| Narrow camera, glass UI | Yes (Inter is SIL OFL; we keep our self-hosted fonts) | Narrower follow-camera lens; darker glass panels and pill controls in the step panel. |
| SD 2.5 generated art, ElevenLabs audio | Paid services | Not used. Icons stay public-domain photographs of real icons; there is no audio. |
| React | Different from his stack | We keep React Three Fiber: it is a thin layer over the same three.js objects, every technique above works unchanged inside it, and rewriting the app would put the working doors, step panel and walking at risk for no visual gain. |

## Checklist we hold this walkthrough to

Each item is checked in the lab (`/lab`) and the screenshot run (`npm run shots`).

### Art direction
- [ ] One coherent stylized look: simple rounded shapes, flat warm colors, candlelit gold, deep red,
      lapis and ochre. Photographic images only where they are the content (the icons).
- [ ] People are clearly people at every distance: proportion, posture and color carry them.

### Lighting and post
- [ ] A readable key: daylight through the windows; candles, lampadas and chandeliers as practicals
      that visibly warm what is near them.
- [ ] Soft shadows: shadow map for big shapes, blob shadows under every person.
- [ ] ACES tone mapping without clipped whites; bloom only on flames, windows and bright gilt.
- [ ] Ambient occlusion in corners; depth of field that keeps the subject sharp.
- [ ] Incense haze and light shafts at low opacity.

### People
- [ ] Procedural figures only: no downloaded character models.
- [ ] Proportions: head about 1/7.5 of height; adult heights vary about ±6%; children and elders.
- [ ] Variety: at least 6 skin tones, 6 hair colors, 8 clothing colors; women in skirts, some with
      headscarves; men in jackets.
- [ ] Clergy unmistakable: priest in a Greek-cut phelonion over a sticharion with the epitrachelion
      showing; deacon in a sticharion with the orarion over the left shoulder; servers in sticharia.
- [ ] Feet on the floor, nobody clipping pews; postures fit the step (stand, sit, bow, kneel, sign
      of the cross, arms crossed at communion).
- [ ] The whole congregation draws in a handful of calls.

### Camera and interaction
- [ ] Every step has a composed view; transitions ease; free-look pitch stays clamped.
- [ ] Doors: at "Holy things" the royal doors are open for the faithful and shut for the clergy's
      communion.

### Performance
- [ ] Medium holds 30 fps on a mid-range laptop; Low holds 30 fps on a phone.
- [ ] JS bundle within budget; first view within the load budget.
